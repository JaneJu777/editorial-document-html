# Newsroom article style

This is an original single-document newsroom system built from broad editorial principles: cream paper, compact hierarchy, ruled grids, serif reading type, and restrained utility controls. It does not reproduce or imply affiliation with any third-party publication or product.

## Visual decisions

- Cream paper `#fdfcf3`, near-black ink, muted metadata, and `#d9d9d9` hairline rules. Depth comes from thin borders and tonal surfaces, not shadows.
- **Only two rules are drawn in ink**: the rule directly above a chapter title (`.article-chapter h2`) and the table's outer frame (`.table-scroll`). Every other divider — hero, summary, chapter-to-chapter, cards, comparison panels, aside, callout, link list, table header and cells, blockquote — is the `--line` hairline. Black hairlines scattered through the page read as heavy; keep the ink for the two structural anchors only.
- Editorial copy uses a serif stack. The reference names Plantin, but the skill does not distribute that proprietary font. It tries a locally installed Plantin, then Source Serif 4, Noto Serif SC, Georgia, and generic serif. Utility controls use a restrained sans-serif stack.
- Headline and chapter typography is compact and left aligned. Small labels are tracked uppercase where the language supports it. For Chinese, do not force uppercase or awkward letter spacing into the source text.
- The chapter numeral beside a chapter title is display type, not a utility label: it uses the newsroom serif stack (`--news-serif`) at `38px` (`30px` under 700px) and must stay **larger than the section numeral** (`2.1`), which inherits the `24px` of its `h3`. A small caps numeral beside a 32px title is wrong here.
- Three-column card grid at desktop width, single column on narrow screens. Cards are unfilled and divided by hairlines by default, so a `tone` needs its own monochrome step to show at all: `surface` = white, `tint` = the cream highlight `--soft`, `ink` = an ink panel with `--page` text, `accent` = the cream highlight boxed by 3px ink rules top and bottom. Card emoji are not rendered — that is an `editorial` convention.
- Comparison panels are ruled and sit on two different grounds: `tone-old` on the cream highlight `--soft`, `tone-new` and `tone-accent` on white (`var(--table)` in dark). Nothing else's tint may leak in — `editorial`'s cool `#e7f4f8`, for instance, would introduce a second categorical palette.
- The whole card family follows the grid's print rules: square corners, hairlines instead of boxes, and no gutters between sibling panels. `compare` closes with a full-width hairline top and bottom and splits its panels with a single 1px rule; `aside` and `callout` are flat cream fields closed by hairlines top and bottom; the link list is a ruled single-column index — **one link per row**, never a two-up grid — with underlined titles and sans paths. Nothing in the family takes a radius, a shadow, or an all-round border — a card that looks like a rounded box is wrong in this style.
- Micro labels (`card-label`, `kv-list dt`, link notes, aside meta) are sans caps, matching the eyebrow and chip labels. Body copy inside cards stays in the serif stack.
- Tables are boxed in ink: the scroll frame takes `--ink` as its border colour and the header row uses the pale cream `--table-head` (`#fdfbe4` light, `#302d25` dark) with a `--line` hairline under it. Neither an ink-filled header (too heavy at this size) nor a black header rule (too heavy next to the cream) is wanted. The cells keep `--line` hairlines.
- Tables remain semantic and keyboard-scrollable. Header and row-header cells stay sticky inside a capped-height scroll area. Chips keep their text labels but switch to monochrome outlines rather than introducing a second categorical color palette.
- Flow steps use rectangular panels and numeric labels, maintaining an ordered-list reading order.
- The template does not insert subscription prompts, radio modules, publication logos, fabricated photos, or decorative accents merely to imitate a publication. If source content contains editorial imagery, keep it with its captions; do not invent imagery.

The default editorial style remains unchanged. Selecting `newsroom` changes presentation only: title, summary, chapters, lists, cards, tables, chips, flow, TOC, and top controls all retain the same content semantics.
